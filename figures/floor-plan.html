Knowing Eye: A Web-Based Examination Platform with Behavior Monitoring Using Facial and Postural Analysis
Examination Room Floor Plan & Proctoring Flow
Shown at 1 proctor : 20 candidate workstations - the ideal capacity for a standard-size computer laboratory, balancing proctor oversight with efficient use of AI-assisted monitoring. The layout is modular: it scales down to as few as ~8 units for a small seminar room, or up to 40+ units for a large lab, by adding or removing rows/columns. The proctoring flow described below is identical regardless of room size.
Exam Session Flow (numbered on both diagrams below)
  1. 1Student logs in & completes face-ID calibration at their workstation
  2. 2Live webcam captures facial & postural behavior throughout the exam
  3. 3Behavior data streams in real time to the AI Behavior Analysis Engine
  4. 4Engine flags anomalies (no-face, multiple faces, poor posture, etc.) to the Proctor Dashboard
  5. 5Proctor reviews the alert and intervenes on-site if warranted
  6. 6Student submits the exam; session log & behavior report are finalized

Variation A - Rows Facing Front (recommended default) - 20 workstations, 4 rows x 5 columns
Best for: general use. Familiar row layout, clear proctor sightlines down every aisle, and the simplest to scale by adding rows or columns.
Variation B - Perimeter / Anti-Collusion Layout - 20 workstations around the walls, proctor centered
Best for: high-stakes exams. Facing the walls removes line-of-sight between candidates, so peer copying drops without relying on the proctor watching every screen directly - oversight instead comes from the centralized AI dashboard, which is the point of this system.
