Discussion

The Level 1 Data Flow Diagram decomposes the single P0 process from the Context Diagram into the six major sub-processes that actually exist in the Knowing Eye codebase, one per Django feature app (backend/features/*): 1.0 Authentication & Access Management, 2.0 Exam & Content Management, 3.0 Exam Session & Response Handling, 4.0 Identity & Frame Monitoring, 5.0 Behavior Analysis & Alerting, and 6.0 Reporting & Analytics. The four external entities carry over unchanged from Level 0, but their flows now terminate at the specific sub-process that actually handles them instead of the system as a whole.

Five data stores appear because five of the six processes own persisted state: D1 User & Access Store (the authentication app's User, EmailVerification, and PermissionChange tables), D2 Exam & Question Bank (exams: Exam, ExamSection, Question, QuestionPool, ExamAssignment), D3 Session & Response Store (session: ExamSession, Response, SessionLog), D4 Identity Reference Store (monitoring: SessionIdentityReference), and D5 Behavior & Alert Log (behavior: BehaviorLog, Alert). 6.0 Reporting & Analytics deliberately owns no store of its own - it is a read-only aggregator that pulls from D2, D3, and D5 to build the Examiner/Admin's dashboards, analytics reports, and CSV exports, which mirrors how the reports app has no models of its own in the codebase.

The diagram also traces the exam-taking flow end to end: 2.0 hands assigned exam and question content to 3.0 once a session starts; 3.0 exposes the active session as an identity reference to 4.0, which ingests the examinee's live webcam stream (including the initial face-enrollment frames) and forwards frame batches with identity-match status to 5.0 for facial and postural behavior analysis. Rather than pushing alerts straight to the Examiner/Admin, 5.0 logs them to D5, and 6.0 surfaces them through the same dashboard/report channel as every other metric - one reporting pathway instead of a second, redundant alert channel.
