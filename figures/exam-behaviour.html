Discussion

The proposed Exam Behavior Index (EBI) represents the overall observed examination-monitoring state of an examinee using four measurable indicators: face presence, face identity consistency, upper-body presence, and looking-away compliance. Automated online proctoring research commonly combines multiple visual cues, including face detection, identity or spoofing verification, head-pose analysis, and gaze-related behavior, rather than relying on a single signal to characterize examination conditions [1], [2]. The proposed index follows the same multi-indicator principle while remaining limited to the visual features implemented by the Knowing Eye system.

The four indicators are treated as formative indicators of the EBI. In formative measurement, the observed indicators collectively contribute to the definition of a composite construct and are not required to be interchangeable or strongly correlated [3], [4]. This is appropriate because the four measurements capture different aspects of examination monitoring. An examinee may remain identifiable while looking away from the examination screen, or may maintain an appropriate gaze while temporarily moving outside the camera's observable region. Consequently, each indicator provides distinct information and contributes to the overall index.

Indicator Definition

Indicator Meaning Normalized direction
Face Presence (Fₚ) Measures whether the examinee's face remains observable within the camera frame. Higher = greater presence compliance
Face Identity (Fᵢ) Measures consistency between the observed face and the enrolled/reference identity. Higher = stronger identity consistency
Upper-Body Presence (Uₚ) Measures whether the expected upper-body region remains visible within the monitored frame. Higher = greater frame compliance
Looking-Away Compliance (G𝒸) Represents gaze/orientation compliance after converting looking-away behavior into a positive compliance scale. Higher = less looking away

Composite Calculation

Before aggregation, each indicator is normalized to a common scale from 0 to 1, where larger values represent greater conformity with the predefined examination conditions. Because looking away is an adverse event, its raw measure should be transformed into a compliance score rather than averaged directly as a positive quantity. For example, if L represents a normalized looking-away score, the corresponding compliance term may be represented as G𝒸 = 1 − L.

EBI = (Fₚ + Fᵢ + Uₚ + G𝒸) / 4

Under the proposed equal-weight formulation, the resulting EBI ranges from 0 to 1. A value closer to 1 represents greater conformity with the predefined visual monitoring conditions, whereas a lower value indicates greater deviation across one or more monitored indicators. The arithmetic mean is suitable as an initial index-construction method when the indicators have been normalized to the same direction and scale and the equal-weighting decision is theoretically justified [3].

Interpretation note. The EBI should be interpreted as a monitoring index rather than as a direct measure of cheating. A lower score indicates deviation from specified visual examination conditions, but such deviation can have multiple causes, including ordinary movement, temporary occlusion, camera framing, or changes in gaze. The index therefore supports monitoring and event detection rather than serving as standalone proof of academic misconduct.

Important Measurement Consideration

Face presence and face identity require careful handling because an absent face does not necessarily provide evidence of an incorrect identity. If the face is not observable, identity may be not evaluated rather than automatically assigned a score of zero. Otherwise, a single absence event could be counted twice through both face-presence and identity indicators. The implemented aggregation rule should therefore explicitly define when identity is evaluated and how missing observations are treated.

The equal weighting used in this formulation is a methodological design decision, not evidence that the four indicators have objectively identical importance. Future validation may compare this initial formulation with alternative weighting approaches based on expert assessment, empirical event frequencies, or relationships with human-labeled examination behavior. Such validation would strengthen the interpretation of the composite index beyond simple mathematical aggregation.

Recommended Validation Strategy

To establish empirical support for the EBI, system-generated index values may be compared against an external criterion such as expert- or human-labeled examination events. This provides a direct way to evaluate whether changes in the composite score correspond to the kinds of observable examination behavior the system is intended to monitor. The validation strategy is particularly important because formative indices are evaluated through the relevance and contribution of their indicators and through relationships with appropriate external criteria, rather than solely through internal-consistency statistics [3], [4].
