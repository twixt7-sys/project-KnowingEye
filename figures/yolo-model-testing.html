KNOWING EYE
A Web-Based Examination Platform with Behavior Monitoring Using Facial and Postural Analysis

System Testing — Face Detection Model

This section presents the testing and evaluation of the general face-detection model used by the Knowing Eye monitoring pipeline. A YOLOv8n (nano) architecture was fine-tuned for a single face class on a combined dataset of 3,216 training and 804 validation images drawn from the WIDER FACE and FDDB benchmarks. The model was trained for 100 epochs and evaluated at 20-epoch intervals to determine both its final detection accuracy and its learning behaviour over time. Four standard object-detection metrics were recorded at each interval — mAP@0.5, Recall, Precision, and F1-score — and are summarised in Table 1 and visualised in Figures 1 and 2.

RESULTS & DISCUSSION

As shown in Table 1, the fine-tuned YOLOv8n model reached its strongest configuration at 100 epochs, recording 89.04% mAP@0.5, 96.94% Precision, 81.11% Recall, and an 88.32% F1-score. Averaged across all five evaluation points, the model sustained 87.55% mAP@0.5 and 95.56% Precision, indicating that its detections are highly reliable: when the model reports a face, it is correct roughly 96 times out of 100. This is the property that matters most for the Knowing Eye pipeline, where a false face detection would otherwise inject noise into the downstream identity-verification and behaviour-analysis stages.

Figure 1 traces the learning behaviour over the training run. mAP@0.5 and F1-score both rise steeply between 20 and 40 epochs and then continue improving at a diminishing rate, which is the expected convergence pattern for a well-behaved fit — the model learns the dominant structure early and refines it thereafter. Precision stays consistently high (94.9–96.9%) throughout, while Recall climbs from 76.25% to a plateau near 81%. The gap between the high Precision and the more modest Recall reflects the difficulty of the WIDER FACE benchmark, which contains many small, occluded, and heavily crowded faces; the model favours confident, correct detections over attempting every marginal face, which is the appropriate trade-off for exam monitoring at close webcam range.

Figure 2 summarises the final model's performance and makes the Precision–Recall balance explicit. The overall F1-score of 88.32% confirms that the nano-scale architecture is well suited to this task: it delivers benchmark-competitive accuracy while remaining light enough for near real-time inference on commodity hardware, which is a hard requirement for processing a live examinee video stream. On this basis the model satisfies the accuracy criteria set for the face-detection stage and is accepted for integration into the monitoring pipeline.
